## Introduction

Public relations is the discipline of analyzing stakeholder relationships, managing organizational reputation, and guiding strategic communication between an organization and its publics. Unlike paid advertising, which purchases message placement, public relations operates primarily through earned, shared, and owned communication channels—building credibility through transparency, media relationships, and authentic stakeholder dialogue. Studying PR equips you with the analytical frameworks and tactical skills needed to influence public discourse, navigate conflicting interests, and safeguard institutional trust.

This curriculum is designed for absolute beginners studying independently. You do not need prior training in communications, journalism, or corporate management to begin. It deliberately focuses on the core competencies shared by all public relations professionals, setting aside specialized subfields until you have mastered the foundational process.

### How to Navigate the Curriculum

The curriculum follows a deliberate pedagogical sequence, moving from foundational models to tactical production, strategic architecture, and high-stakes problem solving:

- **Start with Foundations:** Begin with *Foundations of Public Relations* to establish the core vocabulary of the field, understand the distinction between PR and marketing, study Grunig's models of communication, and internalize professional ethical codes.
- **Master the Mechanics and Strategy:** Progress directly to *Writing for Public Relations* and *Strategic Planning and Campaign Management*. Tactical writing—such as press releases, pitch emails, and backgrounders written in AP style—is the essential operational currency of the profession, while strategic planning provides the systematic architecture (such as the RACE and ROPE frameworks) necessary to run targeted campaigns.
- **Engage External Channels:** Move into *Media Relations and Digital Public Relations* to apply your writing and planning skills to third-party newsrooms, digital communities, and the broader PESO (Paid, Earned, Shared, Owned) media ecosystem.
- **Synthesize Under Pressure:** Conclude with *Crisis Communication and Reputation Management*. Crisis management functions as the culminating test of PR competence, requiring you to integrate stakeholder analysis, rapid message construction, ethical decision-making, and media engagement under acute pressure.

### Moving Beyond the Core

This document covers only the mandatory undergraduate core of the discipline. Once you have built this baseline, you can branch out into specialized domains—such as investor relations, public affairs lobbying, and internal employee communication—through the [Advanced Topics](advanced_topics.md) curriculum. To apply these concepts by building tangible portfolio deliverables like campaign proposals, crisis playbooks, and media kits, consult the [Projects](projects.md) guide.

## Curriculum

### Foundations of Public Relations

The history, theory, and core concepts of public relations: how PR differs from advertising and marketing, the four-step PR process (research, planning, implementation, evaluation), ethical frameworks, and the role of PR in modern organizations.

[Principles of Public Relations (Coursera / University of Colorado Boulder)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/principles-of-public-relations) - A concise introductory video course covering foundational PR models, the four-step process, corporate social responsibility, and ethical codes; audit mode is free.

[COMM411: Public Relations (Saylor Academy)](https://learn.saylor.org/course/comm411) - An alternative or companion to the Coursera course, offering a comprehensive self-paced syllabus that explores PR models, organizational functions, and professional standards with a free certificate exam.

[Mastering Public Relations (LibreTexts)](https://socialsci.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Communication/Journalism_and_Mass_Communication/Mastering_Public_Relations) - A free open-access textbook covering systems theory, stakeholder identification, Grunig's excellence models, and ethics; ideal as primary reading alongside the video lectures.

[Cutlip and Center's Effective Public Relations (Glen M. Broom and Bey-Ling Sha, Pearson)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780132669153) - The long-standing canonical textbook for the discipline; best utilized for deeper historical context, theoretical models, and organizational strategy.

[Public Relations: Strategies and Tactics (Dennis L. Wilcox, Glen T. Cameron, Bryan H. Reber, and Jae-Hwa Shin, Pearson)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780205960866) - An interchangeable alternative to Cutlip and Center, covering comparable foundational theory with a greater emphasis on contemporary case studies and tactical applications.

### Writing for Public Relations

The practical craft of writing PR materials: press releases, media pitches, feature articles, social media content, and the conventions of news writing and AP style that govern professional strategic communication.

[Writing for Strategic Communication Industries (Jasmine Roberts-Crews, Ohio State University Libraries)](https://ohiostate.pressbooks.pub/stratcommwriting/) - A free open textbook covering news writing conventions, press releases, feature stories, media pitches, and AP style rules through practical industry exercises.

[The Nuts and Bolts of Public Relations (Coursera / University of Colorado Boulder)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/the-nuts-and-bolts-of-public-relations) - A hands-on video workshop that guides learners through creating essential PR collateral, including news releases, story pitches, and media contact lists; audit mode is free.

[Strategic Writing: Multimedia Writing for Public Relations, Advertising and More (Angie Hendershot, Lisa Loewen, Charles Marsh, Bonnie Poovey Short, and David W. Guth, Routledge)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781032461069) - A complementary, recipe-style reference handbook providing detailed formats, guidelines, and professional examples for drafting dozens of specific strategic communication documents across print and digital media.

### Strategic Planning and Campaign Management

How to design and manage a complete PR campaign: situation analysis, audience research, objective-setting, strategy and tactic selection, budgeting, implementation, and evaluation using frameworks such as RACE, ROPE, and the PESO model.

[Integrated and Strategic Communication Campaigns (Coursera / University of Colorado Boulder)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/integrated-and-strategic-communication-campaigns) - A step-by-step video course walking learners through defining a communication problem, setting SMART objectives, selecting media channels, and evaluating campaign outcomes; audit mode is free.

[Strategic Planning for Public Relations (Deborah A. Silverman and Ronald D. Smith, Routledge)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781032391168) - The discipline's premier textbook on campaign design, detailing a structured four-phase, ten-step process covering formative research, strategy formulation, PESO tactics, and evaluation metrics.

[Planning and Managing Public Relations Campaigns: A Strategic Approach (Anne Gregory, Kogan Page)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781789663204) - A concise alternative to Silverman and Smith that presents a 12-point planning framework widely used across international and CIPR-accredited professional practice.

### Media Relations and Digital Public Relations

How to work with journalists and media outlets, build relationships with reporters, and extend PR into digital and social channels using the PESO framework (paid, earned, shared, and owned media).

[Working with the Media (Coursera / University of Colorado Boulder)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/working-with-the-media) - A structured course covering news values, journalist deadlines, proactive and reactive pitching, media kits, and press conference preparation; audit mode is free.

[Media Relations: Issues and Strategies (Jane Johnston, Routledge)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781742376448) - The foundational textbook examining news production routines, source-journalist dynamics, media interview skills, and earned-media strategies across print, broadcast, and online platforms.

[Social Media and Public Relations: Eight New Practices for the PR Professional (Deirdre K. Breakenridge, FT Press / Pearson)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780132983211) - A practical companion to Johnston that focuses on the modern digital shift, explaining how social platforms, brand communities, and influencer relations reshape the media relations landscape.

### Crisis Communication and Reputation Management

How organizations prepare for, respond to, and recover from reputational threats: crisis typologies, crisis communication plans, spokesperson training, social media response, and rebuilding trust after a crisis.

[Introduction to Crisis Management and Crisis Communication (Coursera / University of Colorado Boulder)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/crisis-management) - A focused video course exploring early crisis detection, immediate response protocols, and how to construct a practical organizational crisis plan; audit mode is free.

[PRDV107: Crisis Communication (Saylor Academy)](https://learn.saylor.org/course/prdv107) - A self-paced, open-access course covering the full lifecycle of crisis management, from vulnerability audits and team structure to post-crisis evaluation, featuring a free certificate exam.

[Ongoing Crisis Communication: Planning, Managing, and Responding (W. Timothy Coombs, SAGE Publications)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781071816622) - The seminal textbook introducing Situational Crisis Communication Theory (SCCT); serves as the essential theoretical foundation and case-study resource to accompany the applied online courses.
